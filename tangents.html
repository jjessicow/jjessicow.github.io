Prompt

The Journey Matters

The 2026 UXC NYU Fall Design-a-thon started from a worry about AI. The more we rely on it, the more everything gets optimised for speed: less time, fewer people, fewer steps. The prompt asked what gets lost when we always take the fastest path, such as the mistakes that teach us, the unexpected discoveries, and the experiences that change how we approach what comes next.

“Design a product that reimagines what it means to make progress—one that values the journey, not just the destination.”

A few takes on this felt obvious, and we wanted to stay away from them. We didn't want to make something that says: you went from A to B, and here is the journey in between. So we asked ourselves: how can we create a tool that is compelling to use and also reimagines what it's like to see your own journey?

That led us to creative research, where the journey isn't a straight line and the “inefficient” part is the work. A project starts with a loose feeling, then turns into hours across Pinterest, are.na, articles and a dozen open tabs. That wandering is where the thinking happens, and almost none of it sticks around. When someone in critique asks how you got here, you piece it together from memory.

Solution

Tangents has two parts. A browser extension keeps track of where you go while you explore. A desktop map lays the whole session out afterward as a timeline you can pan, zoom and edit.

1. The browser extension

Tangents Companion sits quietly over the sites you already use. When you open a creative tool like Pinterest, are.na or an article, it asks “Start a tangent?” Nothing is captured until you choose Start.

What it collects

  • The images, links, quotes and articles you save
  • Where each one came from, and when you found it
  • What kind of content it is
  • The keywords and artists that keep coming up

2. The desktop map

This is the part I designed and built. The session becomes a timeline that starts where you started and runs through each stage of the research in its own colour.

How to read the map

Trail
Solid grey: the main path.
Detour
Dashed: a side trip.
Shift
Pink: your direction changed.
Branch
Green: a direction you added.

The timeline

Everything you saved becomes a card, laid out in the order you found it. The timeline sits on an infinite canvas, so you can see the whole session at once, zoom into any stretch of it, and pan along the trail from start to finish.

The toolbar

The map is yours to edit. From the toolbar you can add notes beside your references, draw connections between cards, branch off in a new direction, leave comments, and remove what you don't need.

End with a summary

End session opens three cards: the session at a glance, where your attention went, and ways to take it with you. Nothing is final, and you can always keep exploring.

Share it

Copy a view-only link for critique or your portfolio, or copy the session as context for Claude.

Users

We mainly designed for design students. We are design students, we feel this every time we start a project, and we are the ones asked to explain our process in studio and critique.

It would work just as well for other creatives and researchers: anyone whose work starts with gathering references, following where they lead, and later needing to remember where they've been.

Process

We moved from a broad brainstorm to a narrow, opinionated product. Each step cut something we liked so the core idea could stay clear.

  1. FigJam

    We brainstormed names and sketched the flow from the first pop-up to the final map, then wrote a PRD to decide what was essential. We chose Tangents over Mapped, Unfold, Trace and Blueprint: a tangent is a detour you take on purpose.

  2. Figma

    We built the visual design system around pixel-style forms, like the stepped logo that looks like a path that keeps turning. Angela Lau led the branding.

  3. Vibe coding

    We each built a different part with Claude, then combined our code into one connected prototype.
